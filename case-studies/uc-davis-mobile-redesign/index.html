01 · Problem

An official app students don't find useful

For students, missing campus events is frustrating, and figuring out how to get to class can be even more stressful. The official UC Davis Mobile App should help with both, but it doesn't:

  • It is rated about 2 stars.
  • Its interface was last updated in 2016.
  • It is full of pages students don't need, like an Email tab that repeats their inbox and separate Courses and Canvas links.
  • Its bus schedule, the feature students relied on most, is unreliable.

Our question: How could we redesign the UC Davis Mobile App to be useful to students, and make its interface easier to understand?

02 · Research

Most bus riders struggle, and the app gets in the way

We audited the current app, surveyed UC Davis Mobile users, and read App Store reviews.

Survey. Among students who ride the bus, about 3 in 5 (59%) said they have difficulty taking it.

Key themes

  • The interface is redundant and needs a redesign.
  • There are too many items for the app to make sense (My Courses, Canvas and Email all live elsewhere already).
  • The Feed page adds little value.
  • Overall, the app isn't useful to students in its current form.

03 · Design process

From roadmap to high fidelity

We worked in three rounds, each one narrowing the app to what students actually use.

Low fidelity: map the app and cut what doesn't belong

We audited every existing screen, noted the problem with each and a fix, then sketched a new roadmap. For example, the Email tab just repeats students' inboxes, so we proposed removing it or replacing it with something more useful.

Mid fidelity: create categories and a page layout

We grouped content into clear categories (like Wellbeing, Academic and General places), added a consistent header and bottom navigation, and laid out key pages such as Student Counseling, building Hours and Bus Lines.

High fidelity: UC Davis branding and real content

We applied the UC Davis navy and gold, real campus photography and real organization names, and finalized each page. The Community tab, which I designed, is shown below.

04 · My work

The Community and Clubs & Organizations pages

I designed the Community tab so students can find groups and activities on campus in a couple of taps, instead of hunting across separate sites.

Key decisions

  • One home for campus life. Grouping clubs, Greek life, workshops and the crafting center under one tab gives students a clear place to go to avoid missing out.
  • Search and filter first. With so many student organizations on campus, the list opens with search and filters rather than endless scrolling.
  • Consistent with the rest of the app. Both pages reuse the shared header, bottom navigation and UC Davis colors, so they feel like one product.
  • Visibly easy on the eyes to interact with on the go. Students often check the app while walking between classes, so I created components that lead with images rather than words. Large photo cards draw the eye straight to what students need to find, so they can spot the right option at a glance and tap without stopping to read.

05 · Takeaways

What I learned

  • Research implementation early. Knowing what's technically possible up front saves redesigning later.
  • Keep the design concise and consistent. Fewer, clearer pages made the app easier to understand than the original.
  • Understanding users is crucial. The survey and reviews showed us that bus times and campus life mattered most, which shaped every decision.

06 · What's next

Where this goes next

  1. Personalization, such as customizable design elements and scrolling.
  2. Built-in sign-in that removes the separate Duo authentication step.
  3. Better accessibility for all students.
