Today's Daily Prompt

01 · Problem

The habit that built BeReal is burning users out

BeReal's daily notification was its biggest strength. Once the novelty fades, that same routine becomes its biggest retention risk. I saw three symptoms of this burnout:

  1. Retention drops fast. The format feels repetitive, and the notification itself starts to feel like a routine.
  2. The "empty room" effect. Posting becomes an obligation instead of a way to capture a meaningful moment.
  3. Low interaction. Less engagement means thinner conversation around each post, and inconsistent posting shrinks it further.

This raised the question I set out to answer: why does daily posting lose its spark, and what would bring people back?

02 · Research

Users didn't want more features. They wanted a reason to show up.

I surveyed a close-knit group of 11 BeReal users about their posting habits and what keeps them opening the app. Synthesizing their answers pointed to three insights:

  • Content feels thin. Compared with Instagram, TikTok and Facebook, BeReal's content variety felt limited.
  • Curiosity drives consistency. The strongest pull wasn't posting itself. It was wanting to see how friends answered the same question.
  • Guidance should feel light. A prompt works best as a supplement that suggests what to post without feeling restrictive.

These three needs (variety, curiosity about friends, and light guidance) became the design criteria for everything I built next.

03 · Solution

Today's Daily Prompt

A new question arrives with the BeReal notification each day. Each design decision maps back to a research insight:

Design decision Why
A fresh question every day Adds the variety users said was missing
Everyone gets the same prompt Sparks curiosity about how friends answered
Fully optional, dismissible banner Offers inspiration, not instruction
Uses the existing dual-camera capture Keeps BeReal's unfiltered core untouched

The goal: give people something to say on the days they would otherwise skip.

04 · How it works

One new step in a routine people already know

  1. 1. Get the prompt. A new question arrives with the daily notification.

  2. 2. Capture your answer. The prompt sits as a banner above the familiar camera. Not feeling it? Tap the × to take a regular BeReal instead.

  3. 3. Post. The capture and send screen stays the same as a regular BeReal.

05 · Validation

Users understood the concept and wanted to use it

I shared the prototype with the same 11 users and asked them to rate it. 89% rated the concept's clarity a 4 or 5 out of 5, and 54% said they would be strongly likely to use the feature.

That reaction confirmed the core insight from research: shared curiosity, not obligation, is what brings people back.

06 · Impact

A new reason to open, post and browse

78% of respondents said they would recommend BeReal to new users because of Daily Prompts (rating 4 or 5 out of 5). The concept points to two effects:

  • A renewed engagement loop. The prompt gives users a fresh reason to open the app, post, and browse what friends shared.
  • One shared thread. Because everyone answers the same question, each day's posts connect, which invites more interaction across friend groups.

07 · Next steps

Where this goes next

The concept showed strong interest and a clear fit with the brand, so I recommended moving forward with:

  1. Refine the research with a larger sample to get stronger signals.
  2. Build a flexible prompt library that stays relevant across moods, moments and situations.
  3. Run a wider beta with both active and former BeReal users to measure real retention impact.

08 · Reflection

What I learned

  • Lead with the "why." Surveying users before designing kept me from adding features and pointed me to the real need: a reason to show up.
  • Protect what works. Making the prompt optional let me add variety without breaking the authenticity people value in BeReal.
  • What I'd do differently: Test with more refinements, ideate a library of prompts, and run a wider beta with both active and former BeReal users.
