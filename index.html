Product approach

How I think through product design

I focus on the decisions behind the screen: the workflow, the rules, and the moments where a product either helps or gets in the way.

  • Design workflows, not just UI
  • Optimise the most-used tasks
  • Ship the smallest valuable thing
  • Reuse before you reinvent
  • An edge case is a use case

About me

A little about me

I'm a product and interaction designer who works on messy, high-stakes problems and turns them into clear, scalable systems. My strength is understanding how people actually use products, spotting where things fall apart, and fixing them with simple, practical design.

Accessibility is part of how I design, not a checklist at the end. Every project I lead meets WCAG 2.2 AA.

Quick answers for recruiters

The questions hiring teams ask me most. Anything else? Email me and I'll reply within one business day.

What kind of designer are you?

A product designer for complex workflows: SaaS, operations tools and multi-step consumer flows. I own problems end to end, from framing and research through interaction design, prototyping and launch.

What makes you different?

I design the system behind the screen (rules, states, edge cases), so engineering gets fewer surprises and users get fewer dead ends. Accessibility and measurable outcomes are built in from the start.

How do you work with product, engineering and QA?

I bring engineers in during exploration, share annotated flows with every state documented, and pair with QA on acceptance criteria so what ships matches what we designed.

What proof shows your impact?

Every featured project lists the metrics it moved, including conversion, retention, task time and support volume. Full case studies walk through the data and the decisions behind it.
